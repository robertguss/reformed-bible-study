# Canons of Dort: source and verification

## Edition

The Canons of the Synod of Dort (Dordrecht, 1618–1619), in the English
translation held by the Reformed (Dutch) Church in America, as Philip Schaff
printed it in The Creeds of Christendom, vol. 3 (Harper, 1877), pages 581–597,
under the heading "The Canons of the Synod of Dort, As held by the Reformed
[Dutch] Church in America". Schaff prints the full Latin acts of the Synod first
(pages 550–580); only his English text is transcribed.

- Scan of the 1877 printing: https://archive.org/details/creedsofchriste03scha
  (the item page states "NOT_IN_COPYRIGHT").
- Schaff's text: https://www.ccel.org/ccel/schaff/creeds3.xml, section iv.xvi
  (the file's DC.Rights reads "Public Domain").

## How the text was made

1. The English paragraphs of section iv.xvi were extracted from the CCEL file,
   from "FIRST HEAD OF DOCTRINE" to the end of the Conclusion. Page breaks and
   Schaff's footnotes were removed, soft hyphens (the print's line-break
   hyphens) were dropped, and line breaks were joined with single spaces. The
   "Art. I." that opens each article became the article's label. Italic and
   small capitals are set as plain text.
2. The extracted text was compared word by word with the OCR of the 1877 scan.
   Apart from OCR misreadings, running heads and the footnotes, the two agreed;
   the CCEL errors the comparison found are corrected below.
3. Each Scripture citation printed in the text became a proof text. The
   references were read from the printed citation, not from the CCEL file's
   tags, converted to OSIS in KJV versification, and checked against the KJV
   verse-count table. Each quotation the citation introduces was checked against
   the KJV wording of the cited verses.

Each article is one or more paragraphs of text runs, and the printed citation is
kept in the text where Schaff prints it. A run ends with the clause that carries
a citation, so the n-th proof belongs to the clause ending the n-th run. Where
one pair of parentheses holds several citations, they form one proof. In First
Head article 1 each citation stands before the words it cites, so each run there
ends at the close of the quotation that follows its citation. Text after an
article's last citation is a final run with no proof.

Schaff sets First Head article 7 as two paragraphs, and only the second carries
citations. Because proofs count runs across paragraphs, a first paragraph with
no proof would attach each proof to the clause before its own. The two
paragraphs are joined into one, as the Latin text prints the article.

Counts: four heads of doctrine (the third and fourth printed as one), 59
articles (18, 9, 17 and 15), and the Conclusion; 19 proof clauses in 12
articles, 21 references.

## What is left out, and why

- The Rejection of Errors. Schaff prints the "Rejectio Errorum" after each head
  only in the Latin text (CCEL paragraphs iv.xvi-p45, iv.xvi-p185, iv.xvi-p238
  and the heading iv.xvi-p290.1); the English text he prints has none. It has no
  article ids and does not ship.
- The Preface, the Sentence against the Remonstrants and the approval of the
  States-General, which Schaff also prints only in Latin.
- Schaff's note before the English text says the Reformed Church's text "omits
  the Preface and Conclusion", but he prints the Conclusion in English on pages
  596–597. That Conclusion is transcribed.
- The line "That this is our faith and decision, we certify by subscribing our
  names." and the note on the names of the subscribers that follow the
  Conclusion.
- Schaff's five footnotes, which give the Latin where the English differs:
  - Third and Fourth Head article 2, after "[in consequence of a just judgment
    of God]": 'justo Dei judicio'—omitted in the translation of the Reformed
    Dutch Church.—Ed.
  - Third and Fourth Head article 10, after "[calls them effectually in time]":
    'ita eosdem in tempore efficaciter vocat'—omitted in the translation.
  - Fifth Head article 3, after "the temptations of sin and of the world": of
    the world and Satan (mundi ac Satanæ).—Ed.
  - Fifth Head article 4, after "by Satan, the world, and the flesh": by the
    flesh, the world, and Satan (a carne, mundo, et Satana).
  - Fifth Head article 6, after "the sin unto death": or against the Holy Ghost
    (sive in Spiritum Sanctum).

Words Schaff prints in square brackets are kept with their brackets: "[not
because we were, but]" (I.9), "[namely, to Rebecca]" (I.10), "[all]" (III-IV.1),
"[in consequence of a just judgment of God]" (III-IV.2), "[back]" (III-IV.4) and
"[calls them effectually in time]" (III-IV.10).

## Corrections made to the CCEL text

Each correction was checked against the OCR of the 1877 scan.

- III-IV.3 (text): "neither able nor willing' to return" -> "neither able nor
  willing to return": stray quotation mark in the CCEL text; the scan reads
  "willing to return"
- V.13 (text): "should tum away" -> "should turn away": CCEL typo; the scan
  reads "turn"
- Conclusion (text): "by its own genius and. necessary tendency" -> "by its own
  genius and necessary tendency": stray full stop in the CCEL text; the scan has
  none
- I.18 (text): "the Apostle: ' Nay but" -> "the Apostle: 'Nay but": stray space
  inside the quotation mark
- I.12 (label): the CCEL text reads "Aet. XII." for "Art. XII."; only the label
  is affected
- I.1 (proof 3): the CCEL file tags "vi. 23" as Romans 4:23. The citation
  continues Romans and quotes "for the wages of sin is death", which is Romans
  6:23, as the scan prints it
- III-IV.10 (text): the CCEL text has a soft hyphen inside "freewill", as in
  other words the print breaks across lines; the scan prints "freewill", which
  is kept
