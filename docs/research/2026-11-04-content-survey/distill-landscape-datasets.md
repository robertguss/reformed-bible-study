### 2b. Comparable-apps analysis (from the report, filtered by our rules)

The report surveys Logos (Reformed bundles), Accordance,
e-Sword/theWord/MySword/Bible Analyzer, the Reformation Study Bible / Ligonier
Grow app, CCEL/PRDL/Monergism, and STEP/BibleHub/BLB. Its main finding is right:
the core of the paid Reformed bundles is public-domain text (Calvin, Owen,
Henry, Poole, Schaff, Boston, Manton, Ussher, Witsius), and the paid value is
curation and scripture indexing. Two corrections: the report wrongly assumes the
app ships Gill, and it recommends Gill, Spurgeon's _Treasury of David_,
Robertson, and the Particular Baptist files in Creeds.json. **All of those are
Baptist and excluded.**

What a Reformed reader would miss most, with a clean licensed source:

1. **Concise study-Bible marginal notes** (the Reformation Study Bible role).
   The report suggests the Geneva 1599 notes, which our survey-geneva-sermons.md
   found BLOCKED on CrossWire (blank conf). The 1561 Geneva notes in EEBO A10675
   are possible but have gaps. **Better answer now: the 1645 Westminster
   Annotations (A36467, CC0, checked today).** They are short lemma notes on
   every chapter, written by Westminster divines, so they fill exactly this
   "study notes" niche. The Dutch Kanttekeningen (CC0 Isidore-Guild) stay the
   Dutch option.
2. **Calvin's Institutes linked from the passages they cite.** CCEL
   `calvin/institutes.xml`, Beveridge 1845,
   **`<DC.Rights>Public Domain</DC.Rights>` (checked today)**, 2,477
   `<scripRef>` tags. CrossWire `institutes` (RawGenBook ThML, TextSource=CCEL)
   conf: "DistributionLicense=Public Domain". It fits "hangs off a passage" only
   as a reverse-citation panel ("Institutes sections citing this verse"). That
   is an owner call on fit, since the Institutes have no native passage or
   article key. It needs the CCEL ThML adapter.
3. **Classic expositions under confession questions.** Our
   survey-confession-commentaries.md already covers the EEBO set (Vincent,
   Flavel, Watson, Dickson, Ursinus). New from this report: Creeds.json holds
   clean JSON of **Fisher's Catechism** and **Henry's Scripture Catechism**,
   both BLOCKED in our survey (see 2c).
4. **Gap-fillers where Calvin is silent** (Judges to Esther, Job, Proverbs to
   Song, Revelation). Our survey-commentaries.md already lists Durham on
   Revelation, Hutcheson on Job, Durham on Song and Dickson on Psalms. The
   report's suggestions (Spurgeon, Plumer, Bridges, Hodge, Eadie, K&D) are
   Baptist, scan-only, or already marked "owner approval" (K&D). **The
   Westminster Annotations cover every one of these books**, which is the
   strongest reason to rank them first.
5. **Systematic theology indexed by verse** (Hodge ST, Shedd, Dabney,
   Cunningham). These are all CCEL or scans with rights unverified, so not
   checked. Same pattern and owner call as item 2. Do the Institutes first;
   Hodge ST next if its ThML DC.Rights reads Public Domain.

Not missed, or excluded: Easton/ISBE and Nave/Torrey are dictionaries and topics
(forbidden); "Ask Ligonier" is AI chat (forbidden); copyrighted RSB notes and
Vos/Bavinck translations are out.

  A39662.

License position: the README grants "the rest of this repo is licensed under the
Unlicense" outside a list of excluded files. Fisher and Henry are not on that
list. GitHub detects no license. survey-confessions.md treated Creeds.json as
"not a usable license source", because the per-file "Public Domain" tag is an
aggregator claim and the confession files named no edition. For Fisher and
Henry, the transcriber's Unlicense grant comes from the edition we would ingest,
and the underlying 1765 and 1703 works are public domain by age. That is the
same shape of evidence as the compendium's Apache-2.0 (N2). **The maintainer
should rule once on "repo-level grant by the transcriber" and apply it to
both.** If accepted, these are the two easiest WSC expositions available: they
are already keyed per question.
